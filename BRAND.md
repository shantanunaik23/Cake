# Two Bowls — Brand & Visual Direction

## Name shortlist

1. **Two Bowls** *(chosen)* — Literal and immediate: one meal, two bowls at the end of it. It's the entire pitch compressed into two words, it sounds like a real hospitality/DTC brand (in the register of "Two Hands," "Sweetgreen"), and it leaves room for a clean visual mark (a pair of bowls, one for a person, one for a dog).
2. **Copaw** — "Co" (shared, cooking, co-op) + "paw." Distinctive and ownable as a wordmark, reads like a modern pet-tech name (Chewy, Bark, Ollie energy), but slightly more abstract than "Two Bowls" — needs more explaining on first contact.
3. **Kin & Kibble** — Alliterative and warm; "kin" positions the dog as family, "kibble" keeps it grounded in food. Very charming, but leans a little twee/greeting-card for something that also needs to look fundable.
4. **Panpal** — "Pan" (the cooking vessel) + "pal" (the dog). Cute wordplay, good mascot potential (a little pan character), but risks reading as a kids'-brand name rather than a subscription service.
5. **Sniff & Sear** — Pairs dog behavior (sniff) with a cooking technique (sear). High personality and great for social/marketing copy, but two verbs is a mouthful for a wordmark and less clean at small sizes (app icon, favicon).

**Decision: Two Bowls.** It's the clearest expression of the "cook once, feed you both" mechanic, it's short enough to work as a wordmark and a URL, and it has room to grow a visual identity (the bowl motif shows up everywhere: the logo, loading states, the portion-split visualization).

## Visual system

**Feel:** Warm, kitchen-table, slightly illustrated — like a well-funded DTC pet or meal-kit brand (Ollie, The Farmer's Dog, Butternut Box, Omsom), not a generic SaaS product. Credible enough for a pricing page, playful enough for a dog-name picker.

**Color story** — a warm, appetite-driven palette instead of default Tailwind blues/grays:
- **Cream `#F6EEE0`** — primary background, warm paper/linen tone instead of white.
- **Rust `#B24A1E` / `#C9622F`** — primary brand color, used for CTAs, the wordmark accent, and key UI. Reads as "roasted," "warm," appetizing — not corporate red.
- **Forest `#365A2B` / `#4F7541`** — secondary color for the "dog" half of every paired visual (dog portions, dog-safe badges), and for fresh/herb accents.
- **Mustard `#DFA52C`** — tertiary accent for highlights, badges, and the "aha" portion-split visual.
- **Ink `#332D27`** — near-black warm brown for body text instead of pure black, keeps everything feeling like it's printed on the same warm paper.

**Type pairing:**
- **Fraunces** (display serif, high optical size/wonk) for headlines — warm, a little wonky/handmade, avoids the generic "Poppins/Nunito rounded SaaS" look while still feeling premium.
- **Inter** for body copy, UI labels, and forms — clean and highly legible, keeps the product feeling trustworthy and easy to use underneath the personality of the headlines.

**Motifs used consistently across the app:**
- The **paired-bowl split** (human plate / dog bowl side by side, or a single dish visually forked in two) is the recurring "aha" visual — it appears on the landing hero, the plan view, and recipe detail pages.
- Soft **blob/organic border-radius** on cards and avatar frames, never sharp corporate rectangles.
- A **wag** micro-animation (small rotate keyframe) on dog-related icons/buttons for delight without being excessive.
- Hand-drawn-feeling accent icons (paw, herb sprig, steam) rendered as simple inline SVG, not stock icon-pack glyphs.
